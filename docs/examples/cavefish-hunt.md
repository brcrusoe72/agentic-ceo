# Cavefish Neural Reallocation: Territorial Update

## What Changed

The "repurposing" narrative is wrong — or at least dramatically oversimplified. The cavefish tectum doesn't get colonized by non-visual modalities the way human V1 gets repurposed for Braille. Instead it **retains its excitatory connectivity architecture** while selectively losing inhibitory circuits. The tectum is a preserved scaffold, not a repurposed one.

The bigger surprise: **eye loss and tectum remodeling are genetically independent modules.** Hybrids prove you can have one without the other. The pleiotropic package hypothesis is dead.

## Gaps Closed

**Gap 1 (brain mapping):** Tectum is reduced ~20% across independently evolved cave populations. GCaMP6s transgenic lines enable functional connectomics — excitatory connectivity preserved, inhibitory lost. This is now mapped at population-level calcium imaging resolution.

**Gap 4 (pleiotropic module):** Killed. Hybrid crosses show tectum circuit changes segregate independently from eye degeneration loci. Not a single module. Not even close.

**Gap 5 (behavioral advantage):** Partially — cavefish don't just swap lateral line for vision. They build a **redundancy stack**: lateral line + fin/snout mechanosensation + active flow generation (swimming faster to create detectable pressure waves). Behavioral compensation is multi-layered and includes active strategies, not just passive neural reallocation.

**Gap 7 (RGC target zone invasion):** Reframed. Non-visual afferents aren't "invading" a vacuum. The excitatory scaffold persists independently. The question of developmental timing still matters but the invasion model is too simple — what's happening is selective circuit sculpting, not wholesale takeover.

## Gaps Still Open

**Gap 2 (latency/resolution comparison):** Nobody has done the head-to-head electrophysiology comparing response latency or spatial resolution of tectal non-visual processing in cave vs. surface fish. The functional imaging data shows the circuits exist but doesn't benchmark performance.

**Gap 3 (molecular drivers):** Shh/Pax6/Wnt roles in developmental reallocation remain unmapped in the context of tectum remodeling specifically. We know eye degeneration involves Shh expansion — but since tectum changes are genetically separable, the molecular program driving tectum sculpting is a distinct unknown.

**Gap 6 (mammalian parallel):** Weakened significantly. If the cavefish tectum isn't actively repurposed but passively preserved, the analogy to human V1→Braille breaks down. Cavefish may be a model for **circuit persistence without input** rather than cross-modal plasticity per se.

## New Gap Opened

**Gap 8:** Why does the tectum selectively retain excitatory while losing inhibitory connectivity? Is this adaptive (excitatory scaffold serves some non-visual function we haven't identified) or is it drift (inhibitory circuits are metabolically expensive and get pruned without selection pressure to maintain them)? Nobody has tested this.

## Organs

The **active behavioral compensation** is the real surprise. Cavefish swimming faster to generate detectable flow fields is a motor-sensory strategy, not neural reallocation. They're creating signal where there is none — that's engineering, not just rewiring.

The selective excitatory/inhibitory split is the other organ. It suggests the tectum isn't being "repurposed" — it may be maintaining a computational motif (excitatory connectivity pattern) that's useful independent of what drives it.
